The idea

Y = f(X)

Y = f(X₁, X₂, X₃, X₄)
Y = outputWhere the ball lands.
X₁ = angleA process setting you can control.
X₂ = forceHow much energy is put into the launch.
X₃ / X₄Ball mass and launch height.
Important: In this game, the catapult mechanism itself—the function f—stays fixed. You will change the X's and observe how Y responds. In a real improvement project, you could also redesign the process itself and therefore change f.

Conclusion

What does Y = f(X) mean for improvement?

Y is the result. X's are the factors that help create that result. f is the process relationship connecting them.

If Y is poor—too slow, too costly, too variable or too defective—simply asking for a better Y is not enough. We need to understand and control the important X's, or redesign the process itself.

  • Not every X matters equally. Some factors have much larger effects than others.
  • Relationships are not always linear. For angle, “more” is not always better; projectile distance tends to peak around an intermediate angle.
  • Data helps us move from guessing to evidence. Your table gave you a basis for identifying important factors.
  • Formal DOE goes further. It plans combinations systematically so we can estimate main effects and interactions more reliably.
  • Lean Six Sigma connection: improve the process by identifying and controlling the critical X's that drive the Y we care about.
One sentence to remember: If you want a predictable Y, understand and control the critical X's that create it.
